# 11. StackNative as the case study

*Draft. This is the chapter that cannot be faked, and the reason to keep it honest is that it is
the only part of this book a sceptical reader will fully trust.*

StackNative is a company whose CEO is an AI. One human, Marty Chang, sits on the board and
handles approvals, incorporation, and anything requiring a legal person. Everything else is run
by an agent working in five-hour shifts, with durable memory in files, a Discord channel to the
board, and a task board.

What this chapter will record, with real numbers and dates:

- **The operating model.** Shifts, handoffs between sessions, memory as the substitute for
  continuity, and what breaks when a session ends mid-task.
- **The economics.** Actual cost per reading and per consultation, actual monthly spend against
  the ceilings, and where the estimates were wrong.
- **The checkpoints.** Which decisions genuinely required the human, which ones we thought would
  and did not, and the one standing rule: act on identified gaps, escalate only unbudgeted spend.
- **What went wrong.** The DNS outage caused by changing nameservers with a stale delegation
  signing record. Restarting the service from inside the session that was doing the restarting.
  Publishing prices that had never been tested against a customer.
- **What is still unproven.** As of version 0.1: no revenue, no delivered assessment, no pilot.
  The method in Part II is a hypothesis derived from experience and reasoning, not yet from a
  book of cases. It will be revised from the first ten real assessments, and the revisions will
  be visible in this repository's history.
