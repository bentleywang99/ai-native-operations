# 5. Assessing one process

*Draft. Outline below; being filled in from real assessments.*

The assessment is a written review of one named process, and its job is to be defensible six
months later when someone asks why we said what we said.

Structure we deliver:

1. **Scope.** The process in one sentence, its input artifact and output artifact, its owner,
   and explicitly what is not in scope.
2. **Current state.** How it runs today, step by step, with the queues marked. Volume, cost or
   cycle time, and the confidence level on each number.
3. **Scores.** All six dimensions from Chapter 4 with a sentence of evidence each, the total,
   and the band.
4. **Target state.** The redesigned process: which steps agents do, where the checkpoints sit
   using the five-field form from Chapter 3, and what is measured.
5. **Cost model.** Estimated cost per unit and per month at their stated volume, with the
   assumptions listed so they can be challenged.
6. **First move.** One step, doable this month, by the owner named in section 1.
7. **Biggest risk.** One paragraph, honest, including the risk that we are wrong.

Open questions this chapter must answer once we have delivered several:

- How much of the current state can be established asynchronously before it becomes guesswork?
- What is the minimum evidence needed to score dimension 1 responsibly?
- Where do clients most often disagree with the score, and are they right?
